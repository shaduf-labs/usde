USDe Risk Audit / Debt, valuation and repayment

More owed, not demonstrated paid

Two token debts have grown. The collateral still has positive accepted-value headroom, and the lending reserves hold substantial cash. None of those observations settles the borrower’s remaining obligations.

Research dated 15 October 2026 · GPT-6 Pro · Ethereum endpoint: 15 October, 06:13:35 UTC · Includes the 7 October, 16:21:23 UTC comparison · Financial investigation remains partial

The measured account remains open in USDT and USDC. At Ethereum block 26,196,047 it owes 5,871,988.139231 USDT and 10,011.072984 USDC. Its collateral claim still represents 5,359,416.826188716790252832 sUSDe. The health factor is 1.050443628330457118, above one at the accepted marks. This is neither a default finding nor evidence of a completed, fully financed exit. Inspect the dated position.

The earlier partial refinancing did pay the original USDT reserve and return a temporary USDC advance. It also left a persistent USDC loan. The subsequent evidence must follow both surviving debts, not treat return of the temporary principal as the final payment. This chapter joins the later 7 October cutoff to the new 15 October endpoint, preserving what each observation can establish.

There are two useful advances. First, the 7 October comparison shows that both token obligations increased even while their aggregate dollar mark fell. Second, the October 15 comparison adds matching endpoint scaled balances, current debt indices and separately measured reserve cash. Together they make the ongoing financing exposure more concrete without supplying an intervening payment ledger, a complete financing commitment or realized borrower return. An open, adequately collateralized ordinary loan is not automatically overdue. The public borrowing description does not impose a fixed ordinary repayment period merely because calendar time has passed. Neither the account’s purpose nor a private deadline is inferred here. The appropriate supported conclusion is open debt at the measured cutoff, not default, inevitable impairment or a guaranteed capacity to keep financing indefinitely. The amount owed and its dollar mark can move in opposite directions

The additional October 7 comparison

Between the saved 05:44:35 UTC post-event state and the 16:21:23 UTC cutoff, USDT debt rose by 576.905243 USDT and USDC debt by 0.583045 USDC. The accepted dollar debt nevertheless fell by $733.93437306. The earlier post-event snapshot includes both debt prices, so this comparison does not fill the separate 14:04:11 snapshot’s missing USDC price from another date.

At the old prices, increased token quantities contributed +$577.39212882012106. Changed prices applied to the later quantities contributed −$1,311.32650188718654. The price effect outweighed the additional owed units. That is not token repayment. Accepted collateral value also fell by $1,103.45033035, leaving gross accepted equity lower by $369.51595729, rather than establishing a gain from the lower debt display.

A repayment can improve supplier liquidity—when it is genuinely independent

Under an explicitly hypothetical full payment of the selected USDT debt, with no simultaneous withdrawal, new borrowing, fee or other use, USDT reserve cash would rise to 362,820,560.014122 USDT, an increase of about 1.6450515850%. The separate USDC case would reach 138,409,692.781323 USDC, about 0.0072334509% more cash. These are conditional conservation examples, not observed arrivals or facility commitments.

A borrowing or supplier withdrawal from the same reserve must also be debited. The earlier refinance illustrates why: the original USDT reserve received cash, while a persistent draw consumed USDC from a different reserve. Return of temporary USDC did not replenish that persistent creditor. Repayment changes the form of an existing claim; it is not a second backing asset on top of the same receivable.

The collateral endpoint separately holds 104,623,942.252397797865297811 sUSDe, exceeding this one account’s nominal collateral claim. That is favorable underlying-quantity evidence, not permission to release the entire position with both debts still open. It does not guarantee all concurrent collateral claims, reserve future sale prices or deliver either owed token.

A full repayment requirement is not a committed advance

For the specific sequence repay both measured debts before full collateral release, crediting only direct cash in each owed token, the October 15 requirements are 5,871,983.138477 USDT and 10,011.072984 USDC. They exclude later accrual and external expenses. They are not a universal minimum outside loan, an executable payoff invoice, a future debt quote or a financing commitment. The units remain separate.

A staged repayment, assets elsewhere, supported collateral conversion or genuinely admitted refinancing can change the sequence. The 23,908.467042095491407286 free USDe is not worthless simply because no new conversion is credited in this budget. It needs an adequately supported route or eligible redemption before it becomes spendable USDT or USDC. Any replacement funder must remain in the final ledger with its own repayment terms.

For each entity, network and asset, closing cash is opening cash plus arrivals, less payments and expenses. Equal opening and closing cash cannot identify all those terms separately. No new full receipt establishes attributable gas use, effective gas price, economic payer or reimbursement. The selected address’s zero native ETH balance on October 15 is not a zero cost estimate or proof that sponsorship does—or does not—exist.

The current USDC balance is 11.072984 USDC above the historical 10,000-USDC draw, but this is not a complete lifetime fee or interest statement. Its change from the separately observed October 7 cutoff is 10.489937 USDC, a different comparison. The full repayment claim, cash arrivals and expenses have to be reconstructed before an all-in return can be assigned. Another holder’s past fee-share mint, another product’s reward-conversion spread and a generic service charge are not substitutes for this account’s own costs.

Favorable headroom does not erase liquidation and financing risk

The current 90% borrowing ratio, 92% liquidation threshold and above-one health factor are real favorable observations at the accepted marks. The three resulting margins use the same collateral and debt; adding them would count one position several times.

The measured health factor improves by about 0.000101126 from the later October 7 cutoff. Under fixed debt quantities and accepted debt prices, the financial sensitivity places the health-factor boundary at about a 4.80212617% accepted-collateral decline, while gross accepted equity reaches zero only after about 12.41795607%, before expenses. The hypothetical 5% row therefore crosses the collateral-adequacy boundary while gross marked equity remains positive. It is not a prediction that USDe’s spot price moves by that amount, evidence of a realized loss, or a funded-liquidation result.

The corresponding October 7 analysis already showed this distinction: under its own fixed-debt 5% accepted-value scenario, health factor was approximately 0.997825377 with about $496,936.65 of positive gross accepted equity. That older row retains its October 7 inputs; it is not combined with the October 15 row as a cumulative stress.

There is also a favorable conditional countercase. A common factor affecting collateral valuation and the dominant USDT liability can partly offset rather than mechanically compound. Independent collateral and debt-price changes can instead be adverse. The applicable input relationships, caps and exceptional controls remain conditional where the prior deployment investigation did not establish them. Financial consistency at normal endpoints is not an exceptional-safeguard certification. Control and contracts.

Patient unlevered holders, borrowers, original suppliers and replacement lenders face different deadlines. A temporary sale discount can make an immediate exit unattractive without proving impaired backing. Continued financing may allow recovery, but owed-token quantities can keep increasing. A genuine collateral impairment or inaccessible release can instead transfer loss to a lender whose claim cannot be realized. An accepted valuation that delays forced selling can protect the borrower in one case while leaving suppliers exposed in another. None of these conditional paths follows merely from the fact that the loan remains open.

The financial investigation remains partial because final funding, sufficiently supported realization and attributable expense are unestablished—not because an ongoing loan must be forced to close to make research useful. A later zero debt balance would still need its cause explained: payment, claim settlement, liquidation or loss recognition are not the same outcome. A lower dollar mark would still need quantities and prices separated. A credible improvement would connect the relevant liability change to its asset, payer, recipient, time and remaining claim.
