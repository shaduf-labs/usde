The conversion behind a partial repayment

Historical DAI and USDS records now explain more of the USDT that reached a borrower and its lender. They do not identify every payer, settle the last creditor or establish the complete cost.

Evidence examined 17 October 2026 · Event occurred 7 October 2026, 05:44:35 UTC · Last whole-account endpoint: 16 October, 02:58:59 UTC · No refreshed borrower or reserve state

The useful advance is an actual conversion trail, not a new quote. The selected historical records connect 10,000 DAI delivered to a routing address, a slightly larger DAI-to-USDS conversion, and 10,001.640594 USDT paid out by a pool manager. That last amount matches the already recorded transfer to the borrower.

The original USDT reserve received a partial payment and temporary USDC principal returned. A persistent USDC loan financed that return. Complete original-and-replacement-creditor recovery, all upstream funding ownership and attributable total expenses remain unestablished.

More of the payment is explained; it is not a second payment

The transaction occurred on 7 October 2026 at 05:44:35 UTC, on Ethereum in block 26,138,499. The additional evidence was examined on 17 October. This ten-day separation matters: learning more about a payment does not create another payment, increase its amount or refresh the debtor’s balances. The last accepted whole-account observation remains the separately dated October 16 endpoint.

The earlier partial-refinancing chapter already established the borrower’s USDT receipt, payment to the original reserve, creation of replacement USDC debt and return of temporary USDC principal. The new records explain intermediate asset movements inside that same transaction. Several steps previously represented only as an untraced conversion are now supported by matching transfers and financial events.

This is favorable evidence about a real payment path. DAI was delivered, USDS was created and passed onward, and USDT emerged in the denomination the original creditor required. The result is stronger than multiplying an input by an indicative exchange rate. It nevertheless describes one historical passage at the recorded amount, not a reserved future conversion or a facility committed to refinance the whole position.

The audit still asks who supplies each resource, which claim changes hands and who is left facing the deadline. A recipient can be known while its complete funding source is not. A creditor can receive cash while another creditor acquires a persistent loan. An event can report a fee rate while the borrower’s total attributable expense remains unknown. Keeping those distinctions is necessary to understand what this successful partial sequence did—and did not—resolve.

DAI delivery becomes USDS settlement and then USDT cash

The selected account is 0xea1776733cc969df4ff004a9e1a2d87f5b60add4 on Ethereum. The routing address, 0x8f10b468b06c6fd214b65f87778827f7d113f996, receives and passes the relevant intermediate assets. “Routing address” describes that observed role; it is not an identification of its operator, legal owner, audited implementation or service terms.

Eleven newly examined historical logs match nine previously retained selected records by transaction hash, block hash, transaction index and ordering. Together they provide twenty selected records in one transaction. They are not twenty newly acquired events or a complete receipt. The transaction index is 162. Four additional historical asset getters bind the PSM’s USDC/DAI pair and the converter’s DAI/USDS pair at the event block.

A known recipient does not identify every payer

The PSM’s SellGem event calls one indexed field owner. In the inspected source, that field identifies the DAI recipient. The financial branch takes the input asset from the caller and delivers DAI to the named recipient. The field name alone therefore cannot establish who originally owned the USDC, who authorized every upstream transfer or who ultimately bore its cost. Source 02

The inherited record shows temporary USDC reaching the borrower and then a first recipient. The new record shows the PSM delivering DAI in the same transaction. The complete set of transfers between that first USDC recipient and the PSM’s funding side is still unestablished. Common transaction identity and ordering support joining the conversion to the refinancing, but they do not replace the missing payer-by-payer resource account.

A second, much smaller limit appears in the next input. The recorded converter input exceeds the acquired PSM output by 0.000000180475496719 DAI, or 180,475,496,719 base units. The amount is economically negligible relative to the 10,000-unit conversion. It matters to exact attribution, not as an explanation of a materially different whole-position recovery.

The routing address could have held DAI already or received an additional inflow. Neither explanation is established. The difference is not labelled a fee, sponsorship payment, profit, backing deficit or service of another debt. It is a difference between two observed legs—not a measured net change across the routing address’s complete holdings. Preserving it avoids both sensationalizing a tiny residual and falsely assigning every unit to a single source.

For a lender judging a future funding route, those are different questions from whether historical USDT arrived. The arrival is supported. The full source of every input, the authority to reuse those funds and the terms of a larger commitment are not. Evidence of one admitted partial conversion should not be discarded, but it should not be made to answer an ownership or capacity question outside its scope.

Amount, rate and debt service are different cost objects

The historical PSM event reports zero DAI fee for this conversion. Its source exposes ordinary and fee-exempt entry paths that share the event. Without the complete transaction evidence, the event does not establish which entry path was used. It is not a current zero-fee parameter read, a permanent exemption or evidence that the whole refinancing was free. Source 02

The swap event’s fee field is 6. Its documented unit is hundredths of a basis point, giving 6 parts per million, 0.0006%, or 0.06 basis points. This is a rate observation, not an independently itemized debit or the sum of expenses paid by the borrower. It is not six basis points or six percent. Source 04

The actual USDT received must not be treated as a pre-fee quotation and reduced a second time by a newly inferred fee. A further deduction would require evidence of a separate obligation. Equally, the nonzero swap-rate field does not disappear merely because the preceding PSM event reports zero DAI fee. The supported result is partially explained conversion mechanics with incomplete total-cost attribution.

10,001.640594 USDT received + 4.688632 USDT own cash = 10,006.329226 USDT debt service.

The borrower’s 4.688632 USDT is cash contributed to the recorded debt-service payment. It is not a route fee, and the available evidence does not establish its principal-versus-interest allocation. The payment amount and the loan-accounting split are different propositions. The tiny extra DAI input is also kept separate from this USDT contribution.

The temporary lender was repaid by creating persistent debt

The historical transaction contains three financing relationships. The original Aave USDT reserve receives a payment toward a much larger obligation. The temporary USDC provider receives back the recorded principal. The Aave USDC reserve supplies a new variable borrowing that finances that temporary-principal return. The last relationship does not close just because the temporary provider was paid.

The same 10,000-USDC replacement draw cannot count once as cash used to retire the temporary advance and again as free money available for a second repayment. Nor is the difference between 10,000 USDC advanced and 10,001.640594 USDT received a dollar profit. The assets are different, the sequence includes own cash and intermediate claims, and the complete attributable expense and valuation account is missing.

At the last measured whole-account cutoff, 16 October 2026 at 02:58:59 UTC, the account owed 5,872,570.456250 USDT and 10,012.220363 USDC. Its reported health factor was 1.050458478312570382. These are inherited endpoint findings, not October 17 balances. Positive accepted-value headroom remains favorable evidence about that state; it neither discharges debt nor establishes default. The historical conversion predates this endpoint.

A replacement lender can help a borrower avoid an immediate sale or a funding delay. It also takes continuing collateral, price and timing exposure. A complete recovery would require sufficient final resources for each surviving obligation, the permission to use them and an account of what claims remain afterward. No new full-position facility, final payoff or collateral realization is established here.

The October 16 cash-versus-claim distinction also remains relevant. Settlement using an owned matching supplier claim could discharge a debt without adding reserve cash. The present conversion does not establish acquisition of such aUSDT or aUSDC claims. The earlier zero directly held matching claims remain scoped, dated observations—not proof that another account, a later purchase or independent support is impossible.

A stronger event account is not complete wallet history

The new evidence is most useful for explaining what did happen inside the selected historical transaction. It does not establish a complete account of what happened afterward. Twenty selected records can describe meaningful financial steps without being the transaction’s entire receipt, all internal calls or an independently complete history.

An indexed ERC-20 page for blocks 26,141,670–26,202,255 returned no rows and no next page. The provider expressly did not establish independent completeness. Exhausting the returned page set therefore does not show that no payments, liquidations or relevant token movements occurred throughout the requested 60,586 blocks. It also does not establish current ownership.

The older 54,378-block gap and accepted 6,208-block ordinary-beneficiary/owner window retain their separate meanings. The successful empty ordinary-role filters remain valid for their stated scope. They do not become complete liquidation or token-history coverage. Categories without usable observations remain unestablished; they are not successful zero-result searches.

A usable full receipt was not acquired for the known transaction. This does not negate the matched historical logs, establish that the transaction never happened or make it currently pending. It leaves receipt-level fields such as status, gas used and effective gas price unavailable. Matching transfers supports the specified cash movements; it does not supply every execution expense or the outer payer. Source 01

A local success can coexist with unresolved whole-position risk

For the borrower, the historical sequence demonstrates financial flexibility: an owed token arrived through several same-chain conversions and the original reserve received a payment. The acquired legs do not show a contemporaneous sale of the account’s sUSDe collateral. That favorable result should not be discarded merely because the borrower remained leveraged afterward.

For suppliers, the affected reserve matters. A USDT payment is cash at the USDT reserve; a new USDC borrowing creates continuing exposure in the USDC reserve. Neither a successful conversion elsewhere nor a completed temporary-principal return guarantees that every supplier can withdraw together. Paying one creditor can move the remaining risk rather than eliminate it.

For a holder of an intermediate claim, DAI becoming USDS changes the asset and its next conversion dependency. The equal quantity is not a separate legal-recovery opinion or a second backing contribution. Likewise, a transfer and an event reporting that transfer are two observations of one flow, not two funding resources.

The wider audit still distinguishes a recoverable market discount from actual backing impairment. This historical conversion measures neither full sUSDe collateral-sale capacity nor an issuer-wide reserve shortfall. The selected PT’s hypothetical 900,000-USDC obligation is not the actual Aave debtor’s loan. Its larger route and funded waiting-period questions are not resolved by a successful approximately 10,000-unit currency conversion.

The same is true across networks. An Ethereum receipt does not fund the selected Base borrower without an identified entitled resource and a timely same-network payment. Earlier Base, PT and shareholder evidence retains its own dates, positive observations and unresolved full-funding or expense limits. No bridge advance or funded Base PSM is inferred.

A complete receipt or compatible payment record could strengthen attribution of the missing initial USDC funding, the extra DAI and execution costs. A later matched payoff could change the interpretation of a surviving loan at a new cutoff. An actual liquidation or loss event could also change the assessment. Neither favorable nor adverse outcome is assumed. Exceptional oracle, authority, bridge and PSM safeguards remain conditional; ordinary financial events and field definitions are not a security audit.
