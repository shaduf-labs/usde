USDe Risk Audit / Connected stress

When one missed deadline reaches the rest of the system

Trace the first loss bearer, the competing claims on cash and the conditions that can interrupt the chain.

Research basis: 18 and 20 September; documentary assessment 22 September; source-based continuation 26 September 2026 · Earlier observations are not refreshed · Deployed verification remains partial

Follow a failure through its funding deadline, not just its final asset value. Lower income, lost backing, unavailable backing and a bad collateral price affect different parties first. They can interact, but they are not interchangeable—or independent losses to sum. The illustrations here reuse the supplied calculations; they do not estimate current reserves, failure probabilities or a particular account’s expected loss. Stress basis and assumptions.

The same return can be sufficient—and still arrive too late

The 20 September investigation adds a threshold based on observed Mint USDC inventory, with demand explicitly assumed. Hold 30,934,109.172 USDC at Ethereum block 26,019,040 fixed. Assume four separately accepted orders, each for ten million USDe and each paying one USDC per USDe at successive permissible deadlines. Assume no other uses, pre-consumed limits or conversions. These are not observed orders, a queue, a price promise or a four-block throughput forecast. Inputs and conditional model.

Here, a funding bridge means temporary financing, not a blockchain bridge. No Maple or JAAA receipt date is assumed. The amount is the largest cumulative deficit before any deadline, not a sum of repeated deficits or a claim that a lender will advance it. An initially funded bridge brings a matching debt or assigns an existing recovery claim. It cannot repair a permanent principal loss without genuinely loss-absorbing capital. The cash ledger and separate impairment test.

For a claim with principal P and net recoverable principal R, a positive P − R is a value shortfall even with unlimited waiting. A full recovery after an earlier deadline instead leaves a timing problem. Both can affect a forced seller, but they must not be counted as independent losses without tracing the same resource. The supplied balance sheet uses liabilities of 1,000 and assets of 1,015: liquid stablecoins 300, DeFi loans 350, Treasury claims 150, CLO claims 100, net hedged-crypto backing 100 and a reserve 15. Categories are nonoverlapping by assumption; the reserve is already included in assets. These are hypothetical normalised value units, not scaled estimates of Ethena’s portfolio. Recoverable assets and accessible cash are different dimensions. A freeze need not imply final loss; a default can. A margin payment is a cash use in the timing example, not an additional assumed economic loss. Reusing the same reserve for several obligations would overstate protection.

Collateral release can require money that arrives before the exit

Take the supplied hypothetical borrower with an initial health factor of 1.05. Assume fixed short-interval debt and a 5% fall that the actual collateral oracle recognises. The resulting health factor is 0.9975, below the liquidation threshold. Liquidation or repayment funding can be needed within blocks or minutes, before released sUSDe can enter a queue and ultimately reach a separate sale or issuer redemption. Ordered health-factor example.

This is not a statement that a 5% external USDe spot discount automatically enters Aave’s ratio / USDT input or Base’s selected par primary. If backing is sound and the discount recovers, keeping the accepted value stable can protect borrowers from forced loss. Under genuine impairment or unavailable local exit, delayed recognition can instead expose suppliers to poorer recovery. The Base switch / fallback semantics are not certified. Current-design countercase and limits.

For a venue account, a haircut can obstruct a transfer before liquidation. The separate supplied illustration assumes collateral factors of 95%, 90% and 79% against initial margin 90 and maintenance margin 80. At the assumed 90% factor, the transfer threshold binds while the maintenance ratio is only 88.8889%. Those factors are not measured USDe UTA tiers or a complete venue margin engine. Published gate distinction.

A protective decision can change who must wait

Customer-admission and last-look checks can protect common backing while postponing a legitimate holder’s payment. A fund board or custodian can preserve an orderly recovery process while the issuer’s customer faces an earlier deadline. A contract delay, a committee’s deliberation and the holder’s own clock are not synchronized. These are conditional pathways, not reported incidents. Four distinct clocks; external release authority.

Revenue policy supplies another possible transmission path. The 27 August 2026 fee-switch proposal and the Foundation’s 8 September reply reporting passage describe conditional allocation toward ENA buybacks. Implementation and actual transfers were not independently verified. A lower staking distribution can make leveraged carry less attractive; a subsequent unwind may require outside USDC before collateral is free. Income change is not itself principal loss, and no resulting outflow is measured here. The documented decision and its limits.

Calling a backing asset “liquid” or shortening a cooldown does not speed up a borrower, fund or bank. The March cooldown-policy discussion distinguishes liquidity routes, including dependence on USDe itself. A reserve commitment to a local facility is not an additional free buffer, and a proposed token seed does not supply the external payment asset. The earlier hypothetical scenarios remain separate from these documentary findings. Liquidity classification; conditional local-conversion proposal.

A stopped route can outlast the notice window

The 26 September source-based continuation sharpens the service branch without measuring a new outage. Selected immediate revocation can be protective, but re-granting or repairing a route may use another authority path. Cancelling a pending instruction does not perform the payment or restoration it was meant to support. A ready ordinary operation has no general maximum execution time in the examined source. Stop/restore and source limits.

A holder’s relevant comparison is notice + collateral release + remaining cooldown + claim/conversion, not the controller delay alone. In the dated 18 September configuration, controller minimum and cooldown were each 86,400 seconds; those two labels leave no positive margin for additional steps. This does not declare every exit too slow: free USDe, an already-running queue, independent repayment cash or a longer scheduled wait can change the outcome. Necessary-condition analysis and countercases.

Restoration also needs the resource. The later PSM deployment claim does not prove usable local payout stock; a local atomic swap does not prove bridge-independent restocking. An independently funded arrival can close a timing gap, but it must be received by the required entity before the deadline and counted once. The earlier numerical scenarios above remain unchanged and separate; no new portfolio weights, recovery dates or loss probabilities are introduced. Local capacity conditions.

Repayment is a stabiliser only when it can actually arrive

Borrowers repaying USDC can restore lender liquidity and reduce collateral exposure. But repayment may need outside funds before the pledged collateral is released. Issuer redemptions, lending withdrawals and venue margin can compete for an asset in the same location at the same time. Counting eventual proceeds before that order has been respected turns a possible counterflow into invented immediate cash. Lending and ownership evidence.

The narrow dust result in one named Ethena-oriented Base adapter weakens one proposed reciprocal-funding path. It does not supply a portfolio-wide legal-owner or shared-funds map. Therefore no current circular-funding amount or system-wide death spiral is assigned to these scenarios.

The 10 October 2025 episode supplies a bounded historical comparison, not an exact replay. The issuer’s 17 November retrospective reported a Binance USDe/USDT low of 0.6567 and more than $2bn of primary redemptions in 24 hours. Those remain attributed issuer claims, not independently reconstructed prices, summed receipts or proof every holder could exit. The Aave discussion began 24 October, with a contrasting technical response on 2 November. Historical accounts and their limits.

The useful distinction is local exit access versus primary backing—not a guarantee that any proposed support facility is funded today. No full event-block replay, current reserve reconciliation or measured cross-route stress liquidation capacity was obtained.
