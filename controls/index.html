USDe Risk Audit / Control and contracts

Who can change issuance, access and the exit route?

Separate the policy decision, the operator’s permission and the external asset release. A signature count, a delay or an audit cannot establish all three.

Research basis: 18 and 20 September 2026; documentary assessment 22 September 2026 · Earlier observations retain their dates · Not a live monitor

A fixed token contract does not make the issuance policy immutable. USDe’s owner can replace its minter; the current Mint V2 route supplies a separate policy layer. A controller delay, a Safe threshold and a redemption cap each constrain something specific. None alone establishes that every harmful action is delayed or every eligible exit is funded. Control assessment.

Policy authority is not execution or payment

The 22 September documentation assessment adds governance, acceptance, liquidity-policy and published-audit evidence to the earlier control observations. The broader control assessment remains partial: there is no fresh deployed permission inventory, Mint/controller build match, Base transition test or remote-security verification. Read the full documentary chapter.

Governance is described as delegated to expert committees, with Foundation oversight and implementation through relevant parties. The committee page describes three members, six-month terms and seven days of proposal deliberation. Those are organizational rules, not all-actions contract locks or a right for USDe holders to compel payment. Governance roles and process evidence.

The 21 August 2026 Foundation announcement names Kairos, OAK and Blockworks subject to agreement signing, after screening the election results. It is a later documentary reference than the served roster—not verification that agreements were executed, every mandate became effective or committee members control the contract keys. The Foundation’s stated exclusion of K3 for structural conflicts illustrates both a conflict-control safeguard and an eligibility decision. Announcement, screening and limits.

Keep four approvals separate: an investment can be eligible backing without being a direct Mint V2 payout token; an approved custody destination does not establish segregated legal title or fast release; an admitted benefactor or beneficiary does not guarantee order acceptance. External borrowers and fund administrators retain their own release conditions. The permission-to-payment chain.

The documentation does not supply one universal control clock

The served trust-assumptions page describes seven signatures and seven-day core delays and gives inconsistent gatekeeper descriptions. The 18 September Ethereum record instead contains a five-signature Safe, a one-day controller minimum and selected immediate paths. These are different evidence types and dates. No September 22 deployment-specific reconciliation was obtained. Exact conflict and source boundaries.

A proposal’s seven-day deliberation, contract scheduling, operational intervention and a holder’s payment deadline need not start together. A nominal delay does not guarantee that a borrower can first obtain USDC, release collateral, complete staking withdrawal and reach a funded exit. It would also be wrong to claim every action is immediate: the protection remains action-specific.

Immediate authority can protect the system and interrupt service

At Ethereum block 26,005,198 (18 September 2026, 15:11:23 UTC), the tested delay-whitelist entries were true for Mint V2 role revocation and customer-whitelist addition/removal. They were false for the sampled global and per-asset cap setters, stable-delta and token-type settings, supported-asset changes, role grants, custodian changes and admin transfer. False for a queried selector is not proof that every alternative path is absent. Observed selector scope.

The source-supported whitelisted route identifies a target and function selector, not a separate approval for every argument. Revoking an operator can help contain compromise; removing a customer or operator can also stop service. The target contract’s checks still apply. The Safe separately held collateral-manager membership in Mint V2; its full set of deployed transfer methods and destination constraints was not verified.

Both sampled addresses returned false for the gatekeeper role. That does not mean the global gatekeeper roster is empty. Seven mint operators and four redeem operators identified in a bounded recent-event sample were checked as members at the 18 September observation—not exhaustively enumerated across history or refreshed on 22 September. A complete control explanation has to preserve this distinction between positive observed links and unseen alternatives.

The sampled verifyStablesLimit helper rejected minting more nominal USDe than the stable collateral and redeeming more nominal stable collateral than the USDe amount. Less favourable customer amounts passed this particular helper. It is not a dollar-price oracle, a fair-quote guarantee or proof that the helper is reached on every execution path. Those inherited helper observations were not full signed-order or rollback tests; no new contract testing accompanied the documentary update. ABI, helper and implementation scope.

Mint V2’s sampled global ceilings were 200 million USDe minted per block and 10 million USDe redeemed per block. USDT, USDC and USDtb were active in the sampled settings. These are not daily allowances, actual throughput, reserve weights or redeemable inventory. The global maximum is not multiplied by the count of active assets.

Keep staking, remote control and source assurance separate

The inspected staking source has restriction and redistribution surfaces for staking shares. Those are not arbitrary confiscation powers over ordinary USDe. Shares before cooldown, a fixed queued claim and the later Silo payout have different accounting and access boundaries. Staking source scope.

On Base, the direct token owner was an intermediate controller, 0xd896f26f76ed089a1711284a00af497b19d65171, with a one-day delay getter and Safe proposer/executor roles behind it. That resolves an owner relationship; it is not evidence of a historical ownership change. Actual messaging verification, executor, delegate, rate-limit and emergency settings remain unverified. A rejected paused() probe is not proof that transfers are unpaused. Remote identity and security boundary.

Known settings do not equal complete security. Full deployment-matched Mint V2 source/build, all consequential invariants, signer independence and every bypass were not verified. The historical order-reuse finding and the named fixes-review version are audit-scope evidence, not proof of current remediation or an allegation of a present exploit. The Base meta-oracle’s concrete formula mismatch also blocks stronger fallback claims. Audit versions · Oracle mismatch.

Policy changes can affect income and access without creating cash

The March dynamic-cooldown proposal uses 1/3/5/7-day policy settings; staking documentation describes a 90-day ceiling; the 18 and 20 September observations each recorded one day. Those answer different questions. Reported framework adoption does not prove an autonomous rule or current compliance. A liquidity reclassification cannot accelerate external settlement. Policy, ceiling and observation.

The fee-switch proposal dated 27 August and the Foundation’s 8 September result reply concern conditional ENA buyback allocation. Milestones, implementation and actual transfers were not independently verified. Lower sUSDe income can alter unwind incentives; it is not itself a principal-loss finding. Zero ongoing reserve allocation likewise does not mean zero existing reserve assets. Revenue and reserve distinctions.

The 22 July PSM review conditionally supports a 20 million USDe seed based on a test installation, with custody, role, attestor/supply, ceremony and reserve conditions. It does not establish production completion or external payout inventory. The original Guardian audit was not inspected. Safeguards and missing condition closure.

The complete published Pashov report and selected Code4rena scope/dispositions support version-specific audit statements. A fixes-review identifier is not current runtime correspondence; an audit index is not evidence that every report body or operating procedure was inspected. Inspected scope and remaining limits.
